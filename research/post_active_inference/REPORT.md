# REPORT — Post-Active-Inference benchmark (development stage)

> **Superseded on confirmatory questions by [REPORT_RESEARCH.md](REPORT_RESEARCH.md)** (30 fresh seeds, pre-registered,
> 2026-10-08). This development report is frozen as exploratory evidence; apart from this note it is unchanged; where the two differ,
> the research report is authoritative. In particular, the development result that fixed AIF retains control in
> POST cells did **not** replicate, and the AIF's control advantage in the compatible regime shrank from +0.11 to +0.03.

**Stage reported:** development (stage 2), `configs/dev.json`.

* 10 seeds; severities 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0.
* 47 perturbation subtypes in 8 families.
* 1,890 cells, 9,120 runs, 0 aborted.

All numbers below come from `results/summary.json` and the other published files. Every table is in
[`results/report_data.md`](results/report_data.md), which is generated by `python -m pai.report_data results/dev`.
The research-scale stage (30 seeds) is prepared but not run (§ 4).

**Scope.** These are measured differences inside one simulated runtime (`xzr`), with:

* one control task: keep a target region T at a goal activity level that alternates between two levels;
* one comparator family: discrete Active Inference with vendored math, in fixed and adaptive variants;
* one model-light controller and one descriptive tracker.

The results are not evidence about brains, organisms or any system outside the simulation. "POST_ACTIVE_INFERENCE"
is a measured label: the comparator's primitives no longer hold stably. It is not a claim about Active Inference
in general.

## 1. Short answer

* **Active Inference is not rejected.** It is the best of the tested controllers in the compatible and transition
  regimes. It stays at or above the model-light alternative in the post-AIF regime. Its descriptive units
  destabilise mainly along **observation** and **interface** axes, and only rarely along world-dynamics axes.
* **The interval is delimited, narrowly.**
  * Destabilisation is **graded** in most cases: 17 of 19 perturbations that leave the compatible regime pass
    through TRANSITION. Crossing severities are estimated with uncertainty.
  * No world-side perturbation (topology, coupling, memory, structural) reached POST at cell level in the
    tested range. The furthest they went was TRANSITION.
* **The "operational tracking survives" result is weak.** The full-stream tracker stays informative where the
  comparator's sensor map breaks, but that is largely an **information-access** effect: the tracker does not use
  the broken sensors. Given only the comparator's channels, the tracker's world-change detection drops from
  0.62–0.87 to 0.20–0.37. Neither system localises perturbations better than chance.
* **Evidence status:** "Active inference is not rejected; its operational interval is experimentally delimited"
  is **supported only in the narrow sense** stated in § 3.10. It is not supported as a general claim, and the
  development stage does not show a descriptive (non-information) advantage for operational-shape tracking.

## 2. What was run

* **World and runtime.** `topology("modular", 1500)` driven by the unchanged runtime. Control starts in the
  saturated steady state.
* **Phases.**
  * calibration: 2,000 steps of seeded random actions, shared by all controllers;
  * pre: 200 steps;
  * perturbation: 500 steps;
  * recovery / reconfiguration: 500 steps.
* **Interface.** Six sensor channels on T and a five-action actuator on T. Both controllers use exactly the same
  interface.
* **Controllers.**
  * `aif_fixed`: A and B from calibration, frozen.
  * `aif_adaptive`: online Dirichlet learning plus a surprise-triggered rebuild.
  * `ost`: the measured-effect rule.
  * `none`: noop.
  * Ablation cells add eight ablations of `aif_adaptive`, plus `ost` without boundary.
* **Paired design.** Severity 0 is the control; the noise replicate is the engine seed + 1000.
* **Regime rule** (`regime_classifier.py`). Five stability scores, each a ratio to the unperturbed reference with a
  noise tolerance. `C_AIF` is their minimum:
  * ≥ 0.8 → compatible;
  * < 0.5 → post;
  * otherwise transition.

  The primary label is per cell (scores averaged over seeds); per-seed labels are kept.

### Changes made after the smoke stage (before the development run)

The smoke stage (3 seeds, 300-step phases) showed that the first rule mislabelled 2 of 3 *unperturbed* noise
replicates. Per-run accuracy and PC1 share over 30 decisions are noise-dominated. The following changes were made
before any development data existed. They are the reason smoke and development results must not be pooled.

1. **Noise tolerance** per raw quantity: a drop counts only beyond the largest |control − noise replicate| over
   seeds. This is the convention of `xzr.experiments.history.assess`. The null check uses a leave-one-seed-out
   tolerance, so it is out-of-sample.
2. **Longer phases:** perturbation and recovery go from 300 to 500 steps.
3. **Cell-level primary labels.**
4. **Dead sensors:** a constant or missing reading scores 0 on the sensor map (previously the score was missing).
5. **Policy identity** is probed at three points of the perturbation phase (previously one).
6. **Boundary** is measured as the share of the actions' response mass that lands inside T (previously a top-k
   footprint, whose overlap between unperturbed replicates was near chance).

**Null check (development).** 10/10 unperturbed replicates are labelled compatible. Cell-level null C_AIF = 1.00.

**Cost of this conservatism.** Tolerances are large (accuracy 0.42, boundary share 0.27), so small shifts in model
validity or boundary are not registered. The model-validity score never fell below 0.5 (model-structure failures: 0).

## 3. Answers

### 3.1 Under which conditions does Active Inference remain sufficient?

148 of 188 cells are ACTIVE_INFERENCE_COMPATIBLE. The comparator stays compatible throughout the tested range for
28 of 47 subtypes:

* **all memory perturbations:** deletion, truncation, access block, history removal, reactivation, migration;
* **most topology perturbations:** edge deletion and addition, cluster split, hub removal, rerouting, dynamic
  mutation;
* **coupling:** asymmetric, cross-cluster, strengthening;
* **multi-cluster reorganisation;**
* **temporal:** horizon compression and expansion, world drift (T↔N connections toggled down to every decision);
* **sensor:** permutation, scale mismatch, partial observability;
* **variable:** duplication, outcome merge;
* **boundary:** noise, delayed update, fragmentation (borderline at severity 1, C = 0.80).

In this regime the comparator is also the best controller. Success in the perturbation phase:

* aif_fixed 0.47 [0.46, 0.48];
* aif_adaptive 0.47 [0.46, 0.48];
* ost 0.39;
* none 0.36.

AIF minus OST = +0.08 [0.07, 0.09], Holm p = 0.015. The adaptive comparator also predicts best (one-step accuracy
0.59 against OST 0.49).

### 3.2 Which perturbations first destabilise which primitive?

This is the lowest score when a cell first leaves the compatible regime (`results/summary.json → first_unstable`).

| primitive | first destabilised by |
|---|---|
| **sensor map** (S_sensor) | sensor delay, dropout, noise, reassignment, window mismatch; asynchronous channels; variable deletion, relabel |
| **variable system** (S_variable) | boundary leakage, merge, reassignment, fragmentation; cluster merge |
| **model** (S_model) | coupling reversal, weakening, temporary decoupling; cut + reconnect; disconnection; boundary disappearance; variable remap |
| **policy identity** (S_policy) | interface drift; boundary reassignment at severity 1 (C = 0.45) |
| **boundary** (S_boundary) | none at the conservative tolerance |
| **agent** | not measured as a stability score; tested only by ablation (§ 3.5) |
| **prediction-error interpretation** | sensor-side perturbations break the map between readings and the true level, so prediction errors stop corresponding to world error. The clearest case is a dead sensor: surprise stays near 1.5, inside the pre-phase range (0.6–2.8), because 0 is a plausible level |

Two caveats:

* The variable-system losses under boundary leakage, merge and reassignment are **interface-induced**. Actions
  land on the neighbour module too, which correlates T with it. They are not a change of world dynamics.
* Interface drift is non-monotone over severity: 0.25 → POST, 1.0 → TRANSITION. Three instant probes alias with
  the drift period. This is a measurement limitation, not a finding.

### 3.3 Is there a measurable transition region rather than an abrupt break?

**Yes, in most cases.**

* Of 19 subtypes that leave the compatible regime, 17 pass through TRANSITION at cell level (graded) and 2 are
  abrupt.
* The two abrupt cases are sensor dropout and variable deletion. They are abrupt by construction: the aggregate
  reading stays informative until the last channel dies at severity 1.
* Crossings with bootstrap 95 % CIs and the share of seeds that cross (`results/thresholds.csv`):

| perturbation | C_AIF < 0.8 at | C_AIF < 0.5 at |
|---|---|---|
| sensor delay | 0.10 [0.09, 0.12] | 0.26 [0.22, 0.31] |
| sensor noise | 0.42 [0.39, 0.45] | — |
| sensor reassignment | 0.49 [0.36, 0.74] | 0.83 [0.67, 0.90] |
| temporal asynchronous | 0.63 [0.61, 0.65] | 0.82 [0.81, 0.83] |
| cluster merge | 0.60 [0.53, 0.64] | — |
| coupling weakening | 0.63 [0.57, 0.70] | — |
| boundary leakage | 0.72 [0.64, 0.81] | — |
| boundary reassignment | 0.18 [0.14, 0.36] | 0.90 [0.84, 0.98] |

The interval between the two crossings is the measured transition region.

### 3.4 Does operational tracking remain effective after Active-Inference primitives become unstable?

**Partly, and mostly for a trivial reason.**

* **Observation-side perturbations** (the large majority of POST cell-seeds). The tracker correctly reports that
  the world did not change in 100 % of cases. It reads the world, not the broken sensors. This is a real
  capability, telling an observation failure apart from a world change, but it follows from information access.
* **World-side perturbations.** The full-stream tracker detects the onset in 62 % of compatible-regime cell-seeds
  and 87 % of transition-regime cell-seeds. The comparator's surprise detects 1 % and 18 %. The **sensor-only
  tracker** (same channels as the comparator) detects 20 % and 37 %.
  * Most of the gap is information access.
  * A smaller part (sensor-only tracker > comparator surprise) is the detection statistic: surprise is computed
    against a model that keeps adapting.
* **Localisation fails for both.** ROC-AUC is 0.44–0.46 for the tracker and 0.50–0.55 for the comparator's channel
  prediction errors. Neither result is informative.

### 3.5 Which concepts are operationally non-redundant in which regimes?

Paired ablations of the adaptive comparator, Holm-corrected over 192 tests, margin ±0.05 (`results/nonredundancy.csv`).

| element | compatible | transition | post-AIF |
|---|---|---|---|
| boundary (comparator) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (intervention, recovery, history reuse) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (intervention, recovery) | unresolved |
| boundary (model-light controller) | **NON-REDUNDANT** | **NON-REDUNDANT** | **NON-REDUNDANT** (recovery) |
| policy | **NON-REDUNDANT** (prediction, intervention, recovery) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (intervention, recovery) | unresolved |
| agent (persisting controller state) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (prediction) | unresolved | unresolved |
| model (transition model) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (recovery) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (recovery) | unresolved |
| prediction error (online learning) | unresolved | **NON-REDUNDANT** (prediction) | **NON-REDUNDANT** (prediction) |
| free energy (surprise-triggered rebuild) | REDUNDANT for prediction, intervention, recovery (other capabilities unresolved) | unresolved | unresolved |
| expected free energy (epistemic term) | REDUNDANT for prediction and intervention | REDUNDANT for prediction and intervention | unresolved |
| latent state (inference vs one-hot observation) | REDUNDANT for prediction and intervention | REDUNDANT for prediction | unresolved |

**Readings:**

* Boundary and policy are what make control work wherever control works. The comparator's free-energy machinery
  (surprise-triggered rebuild, epistemic value, latent inference beyond the observation) adds nothing measurable
  to this task.
* Online learning from prediction errors becomes non-redundant exactly where the representation is under stress.
* The post-AIF column rests on only 2 ablation cells (20 cell×seed pairs) and is underpowered.
* "HARMFUL" entries (removing the boundary improves one-step prediction) are a smoothness artefact. Whole-field
  readings are easier to predict but useless for control.

### 3.6 Are there conditions where Active Inference performs better?

**Yes, clearly.**

* **Compatible regime:** AIF beats the model-light controller on task success, +0.08 [0.07, 0.09], Holm p = 0.015.
* **Transition regime:** +0.06 to +0.07, Holm p = 0.015.
* **Recovery:** the adaptive comparator beats OST in the compatible regime (+0.04, Holm p = 0.015).
* **Post-AIF regime:** fixed AIF still beats OST on task success (+0.09 [0.05, 0.13], Holm p = 0.016).
* Wherever the AIF beats no control, so does OST, by a smaller margin. OST never significantly beats either AIF
  variant on task success.

### 3.7 Is the tracker informative while the comparator is structurally invalid?

* Of 137 cell-seeds where the comparator is structurally invalid (C_AIF < 0.5), the tracker is informative in 93
  and fails in 0. In the remaining 44 the question does not apply (interface-side, no uncontrolled ground truth).
* All 93 are observation-side: sensor delay, reassignment, dropout, asynchrony, variable deletion and relabel.
* The tracker's informativeness there is its correct report that the world is unchanged. As in § 3.4, this is an
  information-access result, not evidence that the operational-shape **form** of description outlives AIF's.
* One-step prediction in the post-AIF regime: OST 0.53, adaptive AIF 0.45, fixed AIF 0.27. This is inflated by
  trivially predictable dead or constant readings, so it does not count as evidence either.

### 3.8 Does history re-entry improve recovery or recurrence detection after structural change?

**No measurable effect.**

* Reactivating removed history, against removal alone, changed recovery success by −0.04 to +0.06 (all p > 0.19)
  and pattern recurrence by −0.01 to +0.02 (all p > 0.12).
* Migration produced no destination recurrence gain. At severity 1 the gain was −0.01, uncorrected p = 0.035,
  negligible and not significant after correction.
* In this world memory decays quickly (0.8–0.95 per step) and couples weakly (±0.25), so history operations barely
  touch the dynamics. A null here says nothing about long-memory regimes (`memory="long"` is available but untested).

### 3.9 Does the too-slow-correction regime appear?

**For interface drift: yes, across the whole tested range, and for every controller.**

* With the action→actuation mapping permuted every 10–128 steps, no controller beat no control at any period:
  * adaptive AIF − none = −0.11 to −0.02;
  * OST − none = −0.11 to −0.06;
  * all n.s.
* The staleness point is at or beyond the slowest tested period (128 steps). The adaptive comparator's measured
  adaptation lag at that drift is 38 [21, 65] steps.
* The onset of staleness was not located; the research config adds a slower point (severity 0.125, ≈ 196 steps).

**For world drift (T↔N connections toggled): no.** The comparator keeps a non-significant advantage (+0.06 to
+0.09 over no control) and its accuracy is unaffected. The regime is not specific to AIF, because OST is equally
stale.

### 3.10 Is the evidence strong enough to support "Active inference is not rejected; its operational interval is experimentally delimited"?

**First half: yes.** Within this benchmark, AIF is the best controller wherever control is possible, and does not
lose to the alternative where its primitives fail.

**Second half: only in a narrow, qualified sense.**

* **Supported.** For this comparator, task and world, its interval is delimited along observation and interface
  axes:
  * reproducible, mostly graded transitions;
  * crossing severities with bootstrap CIs and seed-robustness shares;
  * an out-of-sample null check that never fires.
* **Not supported.**
  * Delimitation along world-dynamics axes: no world-side perturbation took the comparator past TRANSITION.
  * Any claim that operational-shape tracking describes what lies beyond the interval better. Its measured
    advantages are explained by information access, and its control and localisation are not better.
  * Novelty over structure learning: the adaptive comparator recovers 0.17–0.21 of one-step accuracy over the fixed
    one in transition and post regimes, so part of the "transition" is already absorbed by model adaptation. The
    formal overlap counter shows 0 cell-seeds only because the model-validity tolerance is too conservative to
    flag any fixed-model failure.

**Possible-outcome check (spec § 14).**

* **A** (AIF sufficient everywhere): no. Observation and interface perturbations push it to POST.
* **B** (both fail under the same disruptions): **largely yes for control.** Under interface drift and sensor
  failure, every controller drops to the level of no control.
* **C** (tracker effective where AIF unstable): only in the information-access sense above.
* **D** (overlap with structure learning): yes, partially (adaptive vs fixed accuracy).

## 4. Next step: the research-scale stage (prepared, not run)

`configs/research.json`:

* 30 seeds;
* severities 0.125–1.0 in eight steps;
* plus 48 refinement points from `refine` on the development crossings (20 subtypes);
* ablation cells as in development.

Plan: 12,750 cells, 55,680 runs, about 9 h at 4 workers on the M1 / 8 GB machine (`run_benchmark.py plan`).

What it can settle:

1. whether the 11 post-AIF cells reproduce at 30 seeds;
2. CIs on the crossings;
3. the underpowered post-AIF non-redundancy column (for that, add more post-AIF ablation cells);
4. where interface-drift staleness begins.

Design changes that would sharpen the claims, none applied yet:

* a less conservative, mean-based tolerance for cell-level scores, to make the model-validity and boundary scores
  sensitive;
* a policy-identity measure integrated over time instead of instant probes;
* an AIF variant with the tracker's information (the full delta stream as an extra modality), to separate
  descriptive form from information access directly;
* a long-memory world, for the history questions.

## 5. Failure accounting (`results/failures.json`)

| category | count | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| simulator / numerical / benchmark | 0 | aborted runs |
| comparator | 0 | exceptions, non-finite beliefs or free energy, zero-evidence inference |
| model structure | 0 | — at the conservative tolerance (§ 2) |
| controller | 694 | per-run flag: success > 0.2 below no control; ost 294, aif_adaptive 205, aif_fixed 195 |
| genuine regime transition | 453 | cell-seeds labelled TRANSITION or POST with no aborted run |

The controller-failure flag is a single-run criterion. It is spread evenly across controllers and families, so it
reflects run-to-run variance rather than a systematic collapse. The regime-level paired contrasts in § 3.6 are the
reliable comparison. No run was discarded.

## 6. Reproducibility

* Code state, configuration hash, machine, dependency versions: `results/run_manifest.json`.
* All runs are seeded. The shared prefix is bit-identical to per-run simulation (tested).
* `tests/` has 59 tests, including the vendored AIF math against the sibling repository.
* Per-cell raw records with per-decision traces: `results/dev/jobs/<seed>/<cell>.json`.
* Cross-platform determinism is not verified. Continue a stage on the machine that started it, or re-check a few
  cells first.
